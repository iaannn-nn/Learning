Don't read Wiegers cover to cover. It's a big reference book. Read it **by task**, matching the task list in your plan, and turn each chapter into your one-page note.

(Joy Beatty is also Wiegers' co-author on the 3rd edition, so these two books fit together well.)

### How to read Wiegers

**Step 1: Skim the foundation (1–2 evenings).** Read Part I: what requirements are, the levels (business → user → functional), and the BA role. This gives you the standard vocabulary for business analysis. 

**Step 2: Read one topic per task.** Chapter names are from the 3rd edition; check your table of contents.

| Your task                                 | Wiegers topic to read                                             |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Handling a change request                 | _Change happens_ (change control) and the traceability chapter    |
| Eliciting requirements                    | _Requirements elicitation_ and _Hearing the voice of the user_    |
| Writing FR and AC                         | _Writing excellent requirements_ and the _business rules_ chapter |
| Conflicting requirements / prioritization | _First things first: setting requirement priorities_              |
| Validation and UAT                        | _Validating the requirements_                                     |
| Your own context                          | _Enhancement and replacement projects_ and _Outsourced projects_  |

The last row is very relevant to you. Your pharmacy modernization project is exactly an enhancement or replacement project, and you work at an outsourcing company. Those chapters will give you strong real examples to understand the method properly. 

**Step 3: For each chapter, use a simple loop:**

1. Read it, and underline only the steps and practices.
2. Write your one-page note: steps → your real example → common mistakes.
3. Compare it with what you actually do, and note what you skip.
4. Explain it out loud to your husband in 2 minutes.

That way, one chapter becomes one finished note, and you can see your progress.

### How to use _Visual Models_

Use this as a **toolbox**, not a book to read in order. It groups about 22 models into four categories: Objectives, People, Systems, and Data. You don't need all of them. For your kind of work, these are the most useful:

- **Process flow:** how users do their work step by step.
- **Display-Action-Response:** describes UI behaviour element by element. This fits perfectly with your grid and screen requirements.
- **Decision table:** for complex business rules with many conditions.
- **State table / state diagram:** for statuses, such as a case going from Open to In progress to Closed.
- **Business objectives model:** links features to business value. Good for interviews and prioritization.
- **Feature tree:** shows scope on one page.

**Tip:** When a Wiegers chapter mentions a diagram, open _Visual Models_ for that model. Also try applying one model to a real screen you've already specified, such as a Display-Action-Response table for one of your grids. That gives you a concrete example to show in an interview.

### Suggested order

1. Wiegers Part I (foundation)
2. Change control + the _Change request_ note
3. Elicitation + note
4. Writing requirements + business rules + note, together with Display-Action-Response and decision tables
5. Prioritization + note
6. Validation + note
7. Enhancement and outsourced project chapters, as interview examples
